The problem02 / 13

AI is single-player. Architecture is a team sport.

Every AI assistant assumes one person, one context, one bill. Design work is two or more people negotiating a shared picture.

  • Context lives in one person's chat. The other architect gets a pasted summary and argues with a copy.
  • Nobody can see who steered what. A diagram changes and there is no record of whose instruction caused it.
  • Disagreements get silently resolved by whoever typed last. The model picks a winner and moves on.
  • Team plans mean one org pays. Cross-company or cross-team collaborators cannot share a session without someone eating the cost.
Alice, in her chatService A publishes OrderPlaced to Kafka. Draw it.
Bob, in his chat, laterHere's a screenshot from Alice. Service B should write to Postgres. Redraw it.
AI, twice, with two contextsTwo diagrams. Two histories. No decision log.
Vendors have noticed: Claude Design group chat, Microsoft Copilot Cowork, GitHub Copilot in Teams, Mixus all shipped multiplayer variants in 2026.
The idea03 / 13

One session. Many voices. One shared context.

Alice · runs on her Anthropic keyService A publishes an OrderPlaced event to Kafka.
Bob · runs on his Copilot seatService B subscribes and writes to the orders table in Postgres.
AI · on behalf of Bob, sees bothUpdated ARCH-01 with the Kafka topic and the consumer. Recorded D-04 "Kafka is the event bus" (Alice) and D-05 "orders persisted in Postgres" (Bob). Want an outbox on Service A?
  • Everyone signs in with their own AI credentials. Anthropic API key or GitHub Copilot subscription. Each turn bills the person who asked.
  • Everything lands in one context. Messages, uploads, decisions and artifacts are shared; the AI holds no private state.
  • A canvas, not a chat log. The AI writes Mermaid diagrams, ER models, notes and decision records to cards that people can also edit directly.
  • Governed steering. Conflicts become decision points, not silent overrides. Every change is versioned and attributed.
  • One command compiles a design document with sections, diagrams, data models and the decision log.
Later: the same session model drives two people building the software together with an agent in a shared repository.

Anthropic's terms forbid routing third-party apps through consumer subscriptions and forbid storing Claude.ai tokens. Copilot's SDK documents the opposite: "Copilot usage is billed to each user's subscription." Tandem builds on the two paths that are explicitly allowed.

Design consequence: a provider adapter layer, so policy changes cost a module, not the product.
How a turn works06 / 13

Many people type. The AI takes one turn at a time.

Batch

Messages sent close together merge into one speaker-labelled prompt so the AI answers both people, not the loudest.

Screen

A cheap model checks each batch against the decision registry and artifact ownership before the expensive turn runs.

Commit

Every turn ends in a snapshot of the whole canvas. Roll back to any of them; fork any of them into a v2.

The AI holds no state between turns. Everything it knows is rebuilt from the session ledger, which is what makes credential rotation, replay and forks possible.

Memory, attribution, rollback08 / 13

Nothing is anonymous. Nothing is deleted.

Visible attribution

  • Speaker colours on every message and every card the AI wrote for that person.
  • Card headers: "created by AI for Alice · v3 by Bob (direct edit)".
  • Blame overlay tints each section by the message that caused it; click to jump to that message.
  • A badge on every AI reply: "ran on Bob's Copilot seat".

Invisible attribution

  • Every event, tool call and section carries a causal chain back to a person's message.
  • Exports keep provenance as hidden comments; the ledger exports as an audit log.
Sessions persist with their artifacts and full history; come back to tune, or fork a v2.

Compile

Overview, goals, context, architecture, data model, interfaces and events, cross-cutting concerns, decisions and alternatives, risks, decision log. Re-run after changes for a new version with a diff.

The canvas is the shared memory; the AI is told to put substance in artifacts and keep chat short.

Decisions needed13 / 13

Six choices unblock the prototype.

  • First provider. Recommended: Anthropic API key first, Copilot second.
  • Sponsor mode in v1. Recommended: yes, behind a workspace setting.
  • Default governance policy. Recommended: hybrid.
  • Participant count. Designed for up to 8, tuned for 2-5. Confirm.
  • Realtime infra. Recommended: self-hosted Yjs. Liveblocks trades a vendor for a week.
  • Canvas feel. Recommended: structured cards with embedded freehand sketches.

With those settled, the technical specification is ready to hand to an implementing agent for M0.
